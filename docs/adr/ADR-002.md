# ADR-002 — Transparent account ledger: Ed25519 signatures, nonces, allowance, delayed withdrawals, freeze/recovery, guardian quorums

**Status:** ACCEPTED (Experimental-v1 baseline, guardian scope updated per ADR-004)  
**Identifier:** ADR-002  
**Date:** 2026-10-29  
**Author:** OwnChain protocol decision phase  
**Depends on:** ADR-004 (Shielded-First Hybrid Architecture); ADR-001 (transparent account ledger, superseded)  
**Supersedes:** none  

---

## 1. Context

ADR-001 replaces shielded-only Orchard transfers with a transparent account ledger committing state through `state_root` in the 170-byte block header. This ADR specifies the account state, cryptographic authentication, fee policies, issuance parameters, coinbase maturity tracking, and opt-in guardian governance operating on that ledger.

---

## 2. Decision

### 2.1 Signing preimage and transaction versioning
The signing preimage for any transaction signed by an account owner or guardian is:

$$\text{preimage} = \text{UTF8("OwnChain/TxAuth/v1")} \parallel chain\_id[32] \parallel \text{canonical\_unsigned\_tx\_bytes}$$

`canonical_unsigned_tx_bytes` begins with:

$$\text{tx\_type}:\text{u8} \parallel \text{tx\_version}:\text{u16\_le} \parallel chain\_id:[u8; 32]$$

- `tx_version` is standardized consistently as `u16_le` (2 bytes, little-endian, initial value `1`).
- Signatures are verified via $\text{Ed25519Verify}(pubkey, preimage, signature)$ (RFC 8032).
- The 32-byte `chain_id` binding provides cross-network replay protection.

### 2.2 Transparent address derivation
$$\text{address} = \text{BLAKE2b-256}(\text{UTF8("OwnChain/Address/v1")} \parallel ed25519\_public\_key[32])$$

Formatted using Bech32m with network-specific human-readable prefixes (`owndev` for devnet, `own` for testnet).

### 2.3 Fee policy
- **Minimum fee formula:** $\max(1, 100 \times canonical\_signed\_tx\_size\_bytes)$ atoms. Any transaction paying less than this minimum is invalid.
- **Miner selection policy:** Miners may prioritize candidate transactions by fee-per-byte (fee atoms divided by transaction size, descending) subject to block size and nonce dependencies. This is miner inclusion policy, not consensus validation.
- Fees are transfers of existing value from transaction senders to block producers, not new issuance.

### 2.4 Issuance parameters: subsidy, halving, and supply cap
- **Starting block subsidy:** 10 OWN ($1,000,000,000$ atoms) per block for heights 1 through 1,050,000.
- **Halving interval:** 1,050,000 blocks. Subsidy halves by integer floor division at each era:
  $$\text{subsidy} = 1,000,000,000 \gg era\text{ atoms, where }era = \lfloor (height - 1) / 1,050,000 \rfloor$$
- **Hard cumulative issuance cap:** 21,000,000 OWN ($2,100,000,000,000,000$ atoms). Integer floor division yields an exact cumulative sum of $2,099,999,986,350,000$ atoms, never exceeding the hard cap.
- **Subsidy exhaustion:** At era 30 (height 31,500,001), subsidy drops to 0 atoms. Block producers receive only transaction fees.
- **Coinbase payout cap:** $\text{coinbase\_payout} \le \text{scheduled\_subsidy} + \sum fees$. Unclaimed subsidy is permanently unissued.

### 2.5 Opt-in per-account guardians
- Guardians are **opt-in and account-specific**. They have no global authority over other accounts or chain consensus rules.
- An enabled guardian set consists of **exactly seven distinct Ed25519 public keys**.
- Guardian scope is limited to opted-in transparent accounts only. They do not automatically govern shielded notes.

### 2.6 Quorum per action
- **3-of-7 quorum (strengthen protections):** Setting or increasing delays (new-recipient delay, withdrawal delay) and tightening allowances (decreasing spend limits or shortening allowance windows).
- **4-of-7 quorum (weaken protections & critical administration):** Decreasing delays, increasing allowances, freezing the account, unfreezing the account, executing emergency recovery to the registered `recovery_key`, replacing guardian keys, and authorizing pending transfer cancellations.

### 2.7 Per-transfer pending records and recipient spendability
- **Replacement of recipient-wide locks:** Rather than locking an entire recipient account, protected first-time transfers create individual `PendingTransfer` records:
  $$\text{transfer\_id}:[u8; 32] \parallel \text{recipient}:[u8; 32] \parallel \text{amount}:\text{u128\_le} \parallel \text{release\_height}:\text{u64\_le}$$
- Each protected first-time transfer reserves only its own principal `amount` and stores its `release_height = height + new_recipient_delay` (default: 10 blocks).
- **Unrelated recipient funds remain fully spendable:** The recipient's existing balance is unaffected and spendable immediately.
- At height $\ge release\_height$, the pending transfer automatically matures and is credited to the recipient's spendable balance.

### 2.8 Maturities, coinbase tranche tracking, cancellation, and reorg behavior
- **Ordinary transfer maturity:** 1 block (spendable in the following block, $h \ge h_{included} + 1$).
- **Coinbase maturity:** 100 blocks ($h \ge h_{coinbase} + 100$). Coinbase reward payouts are credited to the recipient's `balance` and recorded in canonical per-account `coinbase_tranches`:
  $$\text{coinbase\_tx\_id}:[u8; 32] \parallel \text{amount}:\text{u128\_le} \parallel \text{unlock\_height}:\text{u64\_le}$$
  where $unlock\_height = h_{coinbase} + 100$. An account's mature spendable balance is defined as:
  $$\text{spendable\_balance} = balance - \sum_{t \in coinbase\_tranches} t.amount$$
  A transaction must not spend immature coinbase value, even when combined with previously mature funds in the same account. At height $\ge unlock\_height$, the tranche matures and is removed from `coinbase_tranches`.
- **Cancellation authorization:** A pending transfer may be cancelled before `release_height` via a `CancelPendingTransfer` transaction signed by the sender and authorized by a **4-of-7 guardian quorum**.
  - Principal is restored to the sender's balance.
  - The transaction fee for the original transfer is consumed and non-refundable.
  - The original transfer's nonce is consumed and never reused; the cancellation transaction consumes its own nonce.
- **Overlapping delays:** Multiple transfers from an account may be pending concurrently, each with an independent `transfer_id`, `amount`, and `release_height`.
- **Reorganization rollback:** If a block is reorganized out, all atomic transitions are reversed: balances, nonces, allowance window spending, pending records, and coinbase tranches revert to their pre-block state.

## 3. Consequences & Risks

**Positive:**
- Complete replay protection via strict nonce sequencing.
- Protection against key theft via spend allowances, delayed withdrawals, and first-time recipient pending locks.
- Account recoverability via emergency recovery keys and guardian quorums.
- Deterministic minimum fee and fee-per-byte ordering.
- Explicit, auditable coinbase reward maturity tracking committed to the block header `state_root`.

**Preserved Risks & Security Trade-offs:**
- **Guardian trust and centralization:** While guardians are per-account and not global, registering seven keys introduces a trust and censorship assumption. If 4 of 7 guardians collude or are compromised, they can freeze the account, alter limits, or redirect funds to a recovery key. Users must select independent, trustworthy guardians.
- **Quorum security:** 3-of-7 quorum enables rapid lockdown but lower collusion resistance; 4-of-7 requires a strict majority.
- **Loss of ordinary payment privacy:** Balances and transfers are publicly readable on-chain. Shielded transactions (per ADR-004) provide an optional privacy-preserving alternative using Orchard V2 commitments.
- **Scope limitation:** Mainnet launch, exchange listing, and production PoW approval remain strictly out of scope.
